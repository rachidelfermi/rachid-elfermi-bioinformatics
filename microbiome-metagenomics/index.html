Choose the analysis around the study

Microbiome analysis begins with the sampling design and the question being asked. Sample type, controls, metadata and the sequencing approach determine what can be compared. Environmental communities and host-associated communities may share computational methods while requiring different biological interpretation.

Marker-gene approaches, such as 16S rRNA profiling, target a selected marker. Shotgun metagenomics sequences DNA across the sample and can support taxonomic and functional investigation. It is not the same as human whole-genome sequencing, even though the abbreviation WGS is sometimes used for both. EMBL-EBI — Metagenomics bioinformatics.

A practical analysis framework

  1. Quality control and appropriate decontamination

    Inspect read-quality summaries, adapters and sequence artifacts before analysis. Host-read filtering is relevant when host DNA is present and the study calls for it. Record the reference and how many reads remain after each step. EMBL-EBI — Quality control and decontamination.

  2. Taxonomic and functional profiling

    Taxonomic profiling asks which organisms are represented; functional profiling examines the gene or pathway potential supported by the data. Results depend on the reference databases and the analysis approach. An inferred functional potential is not a direct measurement of activity. EMBL-EBI — Metagenomics bioinformatics.

  3. Assembly and microbial genomes

    Assembly joins overlapping sequence evidence into longer sequences, which can support annotation and genome-oriented questions. Read-based community profiling and assembly-based analysis answer related but different questions; assembly is not automatically necessary for every study.

  4. Diversity and statistical analysis

    Interpret within-sample diversity and differences between samples in the context of controls, sampling groups and possible confounders. Sequencing-derived proportions should not automatically be interpreted as absolute organism counts. A clear comparison plan matters as much as the final figure.

Environmental and human-health context

In environmental research, plant identity, habitat and sampling conditions can be central to interpretation. In health-related studies, host context and study design shape the comparison. Results should retain this context rather than being described only as lists of organisms.

Research contributions and computational foundations

Examples include a study of bacterial communities in four Sahara Desert plant species in Morocco (software contribution), a chickpea and fungal-community study (methodology, data curation and review), and a cervico-vaginal microbiome study (formal analysis).

A separate 2020 UM6P internship explored metagenomic gene prediction using ORF detection and convolutional neural networks. It predates my approximately five-year research role. These projects illustrate several facets of microbial bioinformatics; they should not all be relabeled as shotgun sequencing studies.

Build a reproducible research discussion

For a collaboration, useful starting information includes the scientific objective, sample types, sequencing approach, approximate study design and the desired comparisons. A computational handover should explain input data, workflow and database versions, quality decisions, analysis parameters and interpretable outputs. This makes subsequent review and reanalysis easier.
