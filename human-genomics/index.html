Human genomics: WES/WGS and germline variant analysis

From sequencing reads to a traceable set of variants and research questions.

Start with the biological question

Whole-exome sequencing (WES) concentrates sequencing on targeted exonic regions. Whole-genome sequencing (WGS) samples coding and noncoding regions more broadly. The useful comparison is therefore about the question, coverage and detectable variant classes, rather than treating the two assays as interchangeable. NHGRI — Exome.

For a research discussion, I would first establish the study objective, sequencing approach, available sample metadata and intended output. An existing variant table, aligned reads and raw sequencing reads represent different starting points; they should not imply the same analysis scope.

A workflow with explicit checkpoints

  1. Sequence quality and alignment

    Assess read quality and the suitability of the reference and target intervals. Alignment and coverage summaries establish which regions have usable evidence. A nominal sequencing depth alone does not describe every region equally.

  2. Germline variant calling

    Calling identifies differences supported by the sequencing data. The strategy depends on the assay, sample structure and variant class. For example, a workflow for single-nucleotide variants and small insertions or deletions is not a complete structural-variant analysis. Cohort joint genotyping and single-sample analysis have different assumptions. Broad Institute — Germline short variant discovery.

  3. Annotation

    Annotation adds context to a variant, such as its position relative to genes or transcripts and relevant evidence from reference resources. Reference assembly, transcript choice and resource versions must remain traceable.

  4. Filtering and prioritization

    Filtering removes or flags records according to explicit criteria; prioritization orders the remaining candidates for a defined research objective. A candidate list is a starting point for further investigation, not a diagnosis. NHGRI — Guide to interpreting genomic reports.

What makes a result useful to collaborators

A useful analysis handover connects the scientific question to the evidence used: inputs and sample identifiers, reference versions, quality summaries, documented filtering decisions, an annotated variant table and a record of the workflow. The interpretation should explain both findings and regions or variant classes that remain uncertain.

These are general principles for discussing a workflow. They do not imply that a specific hospital pipeline, tool stack or diagnostic service is being disclosed here. The Broad Institute reference above is a technical resource, not a claim that every listed GATK component is part of my personal toolset.

The experience behind this work

My human genomics role builds on approximately five years of environmental genomics and metagenomics research at Mohammed VI Polytechnic University (UM6P). The shared foundation is careful processing of biological data and transparent computational analysis; the research questions and interpretation requirements remain distinct.

My published contributions also include formal analysis, validation and visualization in research on HPV genetic diversity across Africa and formal analysis in a Moroccan cervico-vaginal microbiome study. These are separate research contributions, not WES/WGS validation studies.
